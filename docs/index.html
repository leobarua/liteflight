Field manual · v1.0

Plan mapping flights offline, fly them in DJI Fly.

Lite Flight lets you draw aerial-imaging waypoint missions on saved satellite imagery and drop them straight into DJI Fly's waypoint list. It works even off-grid.

Built for the DJI RC Pro 2 and Android 11+ smartphones on DJI RC‑N2/RC‑N3 controllers. Also works with the DJI RC 2 through a companion app on an Android device connected by OTG. Compatible with current-generation DJI drones: Mini 4 Pro · Mini 5 Pro · Air 3S · Mavic 4 Pro

01 · Overview

How it works

Every mission goes through the same five steps. Do the first one at home while you still have internet; the rest work anywhere.

  1. Save the mapDownload satellite imagery and elevation for your site while online.
  2. Draw the areaTap corners on the map, trace a line, or drag a quick box.
  3. Check settingsPick your drone; set altitude, overlap and speed.
  4. Send to DJI FlyPick a DJI Fly waypoint slot. Lite Flight swaps your mission in.
  5. Fly itOpen the mission in DJI Fly, start Timed Shot, press GO.
Photos are taken by DJI Fly, not the mission

The mission flies the lines; the camera shots come from DJI Fly's Timed Shot photo mode. Under Drone speed Lite Flight tells you the longest interval that still gives your front overlap. Usually that's 2 s, the shortest interval DJI cameras allow. Set that in DJI Fly before you start.

02 · Vocabulary

Words you'll see

New to mapping? These eight terms cover nearly every setting in the app.

Waypoint mission KMZ
A list of points the drone flies through on its own. DJI stores each one as a .kmz file.
GSD cm/px
Ground sample distance: how much ground one photo pixel covers. Lower is sharper. It's set by altitude: fly higher and the GSD grows.
Front overlap %
How much each photo shares with the next one along a flight line. 80 % is the usual mapping value.
Side overlap %
How much neighbouring flight lines share. Higher overlap means more lines and a longer flight.
Altitude above take-off m
Height measured from where the drone took off. Simple, but over hills the real height above the ground changes.
Terrain follow m AGL
Height kept above the ground under the drone, using downloaded elevation data. Use it on slopes so the GSD stays even.
DJI Fly slot No. N
A waypoint mission already saved in DJI Fly. Lite Flight replaces its contents with your mission, because DJI Fly has no import button on the RC.
Shizuku helper app
A free app that gives Lite Flight extra system access, so it can refresh DJI Fly after a send. Started over Wi-Fi with wireless debugging.

03 · At home, with internet

Before you go to the field

Before going out into the field, check the following while you still have an internet connection.

License & trial

A new install runs as a 14-day trial with every feature on. From day 8 a reminder asks for a license each time you open the app; after day 14 the app locks until you activate. To get a license, open this screen from the red Trial line under the app name.

  1. Device code. Tap the copy icon and email it to LeoBar at the address shown on this screen. Each license only works on the device it was made for.
  2. License or tester key. Paste the key you get back.
  3. Activate. Works offline.
  4. Load license file. Use this instead if you received a .lic file.
  5. Continue trial returns to the app.
  6. Days left shows in the corner of every screen during the trial.

Download imagery for offline use

Out in the field the RC usually has no internet, so the satellite map must already be on the device. Tap the cloud button ⤓ at the top right of the map to open Imagery Download.

  1. Pan and zoom until your site fills the view. Bigger views take longer and use more storage; the limit is 50 km².
  2. Tap Download this area.
  3. Name it, keep Include elevation data ticked, and tap Download.
  4. The area appears under Downloaded areas once it's done. A small field takes a few seconds.
  1. Name of the saved area, so you can find it later.
  2. Include elevation data. Needed for terrain follow. Leave it on.
  3. Basemap detail. The zoom level to save. The built-in Esri source stops at z17 (about 1.2 m per pixel).
  4. Size estimate in km², tiles and MB.
  5. Download starts it. You can leave the screen while it runs.
  1. Downloaded areas. Tap one to switch to the map source it was saved from; the bin icon deletes it.
  2. My location.
  3. Map source. Switch basemaps or add your own tile address. Saved areas only show while the source they came from is selected.
  4. Size guide. Shows the 50 km² frame.
  5. TERR shades the elevation data you've saved.
  6. Download this area downloads the imagery inside the current map view.
  7. Back to the map.

Use another imagery source. Lite Flight comes with Esri World Imagery, but you can add any provider that offers XYZ tiles. Tap Map, then Add custom, and paste the tile URL, for example https://…/{z}/{x}/{y}.png. Addresses copied from QGIS's QuickMapServices work too. Only use sources whose terms allow offline download.

  1. Selected source. Custom sources you add appear in this list too.
  2. Add custom asks for a name, the tile URL and an optional attribution.

Prepare DJI Fly slots

DJI Fly on the RC can't import mission files, so Lite Flight writes into missions DJI Fly already has. Make a few throwaway missions first. Each one becomes a slot you can fill later.

  1. In DJI Fly, open the camera view and tap the waypoint icon ① on the left.
  2. Add two waypoints anywhere and save the mission. It doesn't matter where they are.
  3. Repeat until you have one per mission you'll fly that day. Five or six is plenty.
  4. The list icon ② shows every saved mission. These are the slots Lite Flight lists as DJI Waypoint Mission No. N.
Reusing slots

Once Lite Flight has filled a slot it's labelled Swap: Mission. You can overwrite it with the next mission; the app asks first. Deleting the mission inside DJI Fly removes the slot.

04 · One-time setup

Shizuku and Wi-Fi

Shizuku is optional on the RC Pro 2 but makes sending missions over to DJI Fly much smoother. Here's what it does and how to start it.

Why Wi-Fi matters even with no internet

Shizuku starts through Android's wireless debugging, and Android only turns that on while the device is connected to a Wi-Fi network. Internet access isn't needed: a phone hotspot with mobile data off works. Shizuku also stops whenever the RC restarts, so you'll start it again at the field. Bring a hotspot.

First time: turn on developer options

  1. Open Settings → About and tap Build number seven times. Android confirms that developer options are on.
  2. Connect to any Wi-Fi network.
  3. Open Developer options (search “developer” in Settings) and scroll to Debugging.
  1. Tap the words “Wireless debugging” to open its page.
  2. The switch alone isn't enough for pairing. Open the page.
  1. Switch wireless debugging On. It turns off by itself when Wi-Fi drops.
  2. Pair device with pairing code shows the six-digit code Shizuku needs the first time.

First time: pair Shizuku

Install Shizuku from the Play Store or its GitHub releases page. Pairing happens once per device.

  1. Status. “Not running” after every restart.
  2. Pairing. Tap it, then follow the steps below.
  1. Shizuku posts a notification: Searching for pairing service ①.
  2. Go to Wireless debugging → Pair device with pairing code.
  3. Pull down the notification shade, tap Enter pairing code in Shizuku's notification, and type the six digits.
  4. Shizuku confirms the pairing. You won't need to repeat this.

Every time: start Shizuku

  1. Join Wi-Fi and make sure wireless debugging is on.
  2. Open Shizuku and tap Start.
  3. A Starter log scrolls by and closes after three seconds.
  4. The top card now reads Shizuku is running.
Lite Flight reminds you

If Shizuku isn't running when you send a mission, the Send to DJI Fly dialog shows a red warning with a Start Shizuku button that opens Shizuku for you. Tap Start there, come back, and send. When folder access isn't set up, the Export screen also has a Shizuku shortcut button.

First send: allow Lite Flight

The first time you send after Shizuku is running, the send dialog asks for permission. Tap Allow in Shizuku, then Allow all the time.

05 · Home screen

The map screen

Lite Flight opens here. The left panel holds the mission type and your saved missions; the map holds the drawing tools.

  1. Mission type: Area, Corridor, Circle or Quick. See Mission types.
  2. My missions. Tap one to load it. Long-press to select several.
  3. Hint for the next step: tap the map to start.
  4. Download imagery for offline use.
  5. MBTiles loads your own drone orthophoto or map file as an overlay.
  6. KML imports a boundary drawn elsewhere (Google Earth, QGIS) as a guide shape.
  7. My location centres the map on your GPS position.
  8. Recenter zooms to the shape you've drawn.
  9. TERR shades saved elevation, so you can spot hills.
  10. 200 m box drops a ready-made square at the launch point.
  11. Undo removes the last point.
  12. Panel handle. Drag it to widen the panel.
Where the drone takes off

The green dot is the launch point. It starts at your GPS position. To move it, tap the green dot, then tap where you'll actually take off. The flight time and the first leg are worked out from there.

This matters most for terrain follow. Each waypoint's height is worked out relative to the ground at the launch point, so a launch point in the wrong place raises or lowers the whole mission.

06 · Drawing

Mission types

Pick the type from the tabs before you draw. Each one lays out its flight lines differently.

AreaAny field or plot. Back-and-forth lines over a shape you draw corner by corner.
CorridorRoads, canals, rivers, fences. Parallel lines along a path you trace.
CircleBuildings and tall objects. Overlapping orbits with the camera tilted inwards.
QuickField work in a hurry. Drag a box, rotate it, fly, with fewer settings.

Area

  1. Choose Area.
  2. Tap each corner of the field in order, going around it. Three corners are enough to make a mission; the banner shows 5 points — ready.
  3. Lines can't cross. If a tap would cross an edge, the app refuses it and says why.
  4. Tap Next to see the flight lines and settings.

Fix the shape. Tap a corner to select it (it turns orange), then tap where it should go. Tap a purple midpoint to add a corner there.

  1. Selected corner (orange). Tap the map to move it.
  2. Midpoint (purple). Tap it to add a corner.
  3. Launch point (green).
  4. Delete corner removes the selected corner.
  5. Undo.
  6. Hint says what the next tap will do.
  7. Save to My missions.
  8. Reset clears the drawing.
  9. Next opens the mission settings.

Corridor

  1. Choose Corridor.
  2. Tap points along the centre of the road or canal, from one end to the other. Bends are fine.
  3. Tap Next. The lines run parallel to your path on both sides.
  4. Set the width with Number of lines (even numbers, 4 or more).

Circle

Circle mode covers an area with overlapping orbits and tilts the camera to look at each orbit's centre. The oblique views give 3D models straighter walls than straight-down photos. Draw the boundary the same way as an Area.

  1. Auto camera angle aims the camera at each orbit's centre, worked out from altitude and radius. Leave it on.
  2. Circle radius. 40 m is the default; larger circles mean fewer orbits.
  3. Circle overlap. 25 % is the lowest value tested in published research. Go higher, not lower.
  4. Waypoints per circle. More points turn the camera more smoothly but use up the waypoint budget faster.
Watch the waypoint count

Circle missions get large quickly. This 2.3 ha block already needs 99 waypoints. The more waypoints a mission has, the more DJI Fly lags while loading and editing it. Lite Flight suggests staying under 150 waypoints, but that's a guideline, not a limit: missions of over 300 waypoints still load and work on the RC Pro 2, just more slowly. Above 150, the Send to DJI Fly… shortcut points you to the Export screen, which warns you and lets you send anyway.

Quick

Quick mode is for short field windows. You drag out a rectangle, turn it to line up with the field, and fly. Only the settings you're likely to change are shown; the camera stays at the mapping tilt of 75°.

  1. Choose Quick and tap Draw.
  2. Drag one finger across the field to draw a box. The banner shows its size, e.g. 181 × 214 m · 3.9 ha.
  3. Drag the rotate handle to line the box up with the field rows.
  4. Drag the move handle or any corner to adjust it, then tap Next.
  1. Size and launch of the box.
  2. Corner handles resize it.
  3. Rotate handle.
  4. Move handle.
  5. Redraw starts a new box.
  6. Save to My missions.
  7. Next.
  1. Flight summary. See settings.
  2. Drone model.
  3. Altitude, 80 m by default.
  4. Drone speed, with the photo interval to set in DJI Fly.
  5. Side overlap, 75 % by default. Front overlap is fixed at 80 % for a 2 s Timed Shot.
  6. Grid orientation follows the box automatically.
  7. Send to DJI Fly…
  8. Back · Save · Next.

07 · Before you send

Mission settings

After Next the left panel turns into the settings list. Every change redraws the flight lines straight away, so you can watch the effect.

The plus and minus buttons

The black − / + buttons change a value by a small step; the green − / + buttons change it by a big step (10 m of altitude, 1 m/s of speed). You can also tap the number and type a value.

  1. Flight summary: flight time · distance · area · waypoint count. Time includes flying out from the launch point and back.
  2. Camera model. Pick your drone. It sets the sensor maths and the top speed.
  3. Altitude mode. Take Off flies a fixed height above the launch point. Ground follows the terrain (see below).
  4. Target altitude in metres. Higher = faster flights, coarser detail.
  5. Target GSD. Type the detail you want and the altitude adjusts to match. 2.9 cm/px at 80 m on a Mini 5 Pro.
  6. Drone speed. The grey line below it is the Timed Shot interval to set in DJI Fly. Turns red if you'd need shots faster than every 2 s; slow down as it suggests.
  7. Front overlap. 80 % for most mapping.
  8. Side overlap. 70–75 %. Higher means more lines.
  9. Shutter speed you'll use. A slower shutter lowers the top speed so photos aren't blurred.
  10. Camera angle in degrees below the horizon. 90 is straight down; 75 cuts the “bowl” error in 3D models.
  11. Grid orientation. Auto picks the line direction from the boundary. Switch it off to set your own angle.
  12. Upon signal loss. Off: the drone stops the mission and does what DJI Fly is set to do when the signal is lost (usually return home). On: it finishes the mission first.
  13. Send to DJI Fly… See Send to DJI Fly.
  14. Back to edit the shape · Save to My missions · Next to the Export screen.

Terrain follow

On sloping ground a fixed height means photos near the hilltop are sharper than those in the valley. Ground mode keeps the drone at the same height above the ground using the elevation data you downloaded.

  1. Ground turns on terrain follow. The altitude field becomes Above Terrain.
  2. Terrain-follow threshold. A new waypoint is added each time the ground rises or falls by this much. 5 m suits most sites; a smaller value follows the ground closely but adds waypoints.
  3. TERR shows the elevation shading under your mission.
Needs elevation data

If there's no saved elevation for the site, the app downloads it when you pick Ground, which needs internet. Tick Include elevation data when you save the imagery at home.

08 · Handing over

Send to DJI Fly

From the settings panel, Send to DJI Fly… puts the current mission into a DJI Fly slot in one tap.

  1. Tap Send to DJI Fly… under the settings.
  2. Tap a slot. DJI Waypoint Mission No. N is unused; Swap: Mission already holds one of yours and asks before overwriting.
  3. With Shizuku running, DJI Fly's cache is cleared and DJI Fly opens by itself.
  4. Without Shizuku, tap Open DJI Fly anyway. The mission is there, but the old thumbnail may still show.
  1. Shizuku status. Red means DJI Fly won't refresh by itself; blue means all set.
  2. Allow in Shizuku (or Start Shizuku) fixes it from here.
  3. Refresh after making new slots in DJI Fly.
  4. Swap: Mission is a slot you filled before. It's safe to overwrite, but the app asks first.
  5. DJI Waypoint Mission No. N is an unused placeholder.

Find it in DJI Fly

  1. In DJI Fly's waypoint list, the slot now shows your mission's name ①. The date stays the slot's original date.
  2. Open it and check the route on the map.
  3. Switch the camera to Timed Shot at the interval Lite Flight showed, start shooting, and press GO.

Send several at once

From My missions, long-press a mission, tick the others (or tick a whole group), and tap the send arrow.

  1. Shizuku status.
  2. Slots available. You need one per mission.
  3. → No. N is the slot each mission goes to. Tap it to choose another.
  4. × leaves that mission out of this batch.
  5. Send fills every slot, then opens DJI Fly.

Folder access instead of Shizuku

On Android 11–12, if Shizuku isn't running, this screen offers Use folder access instead of Shizuku. Tap it, then Allow access to dji.go.v5 and Allow. That's a one-time grant, and missions can then be sent without Shizuku.

Without Shizuku, DJI Fly keeps showing the slot's old thumbnail until its cache clears. To refresh it yourself, go to Android Settings → Apps → DJI Fly → Storage and tap Clear cache. Your saved missions aren't affected.

11 · When something's off

Troubleshooting

The map is blank or blurry at the field

That area wasn't downloaded, or a different map source is selected. Open Imagery Download and check the area is listed. Tap it to switch back to the source it was saved from.

The send dialog says “No DJI Fly waypoint found”

DJI Fly has no saved waypoint missions to fill. Make a couple of placeholder missions in DJI Fly (see Prepare DJI Fly slots), then tap refresh.

DJI Fly still shows the old mission name

DJI Fly is showing a cached thumbnail. Start Shizuku and send again, or force-close and reopen DJI Fly. The route itself has already been replaced.

Shizuku won't start

Wireless debugging needs Wi-Fi. Join a network (a phone hotspot with no data is fine), check wireless debugging is on, then tap Start again. After a reboot you always need to start Shizuku again.

The speed hint turned red

At this speed DJI Fly would need to shoot faster than every 2 seconds. Lower the speed to the value in the message, fly higher, or reduce front overlap.

“Large waypoint count”, or DJI Fly lags with the mission

Large missions still work, but DJI Fly gets slower as the waypoint count grows; 150 is a suggestion, not a limit. To make a mission lighter, split a big site into two missions, raise the altitude, lower side overlap, or (in Circle mode) use fewer waypoints per circle.

My tap moved the launch point instead of adding a corner

Taps close to the green launch dot select it. Tap a little further away, or zoom in so there's more room.

Terrain follow says there's no elevation data

The site's elevation wasn't saved. Connect to the internet and pick Ground again, or re-download the area with Include elevation data ticked.
